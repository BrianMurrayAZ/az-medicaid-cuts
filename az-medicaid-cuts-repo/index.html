Hospital assessment step-down

Arizona's hospital assessment sits at the 6.0% federal ceiling. In SFY 2025 it collected $1.36B from hospitals and returned $4.32B to 134 facilities. H.R. 1 lowers the ceiling half a point a year from October 2027 to 3.5% in FFY 2032. Figures scale each facility's SFY 2025 flows (AHCCCS report, June 1, 2026) by the share of the rate removed — the CSPA one-pager method. Not an AHCCCS estimate.

Workforce · where the providers are — and aren't

Arizona's provider shortage

Coverage losses land on a system that is already short of clinicians. Arizona ranks 42nd among states in primary care physicians per capita, every one of its 15 counties contains a federally designated primary care shortage area, and the state meets barely a third of its primary care need. The district figures below count clinicians by where they practice, from CMS's Medicare enrollment file — actual administrative data, not estimates.

Coverage of the cuts

Stories from the prior two weeks on Medicaid cuts, hospitals and the provider workforce, gathered by an automated search of Arizona and regional outlets every other day and checked against the article page for headline and date. Items marked opinion, analysis or press release are labeled. Stories that age out of the two-week window move to the searchable Archive tab rather than disappearing. Some outlets (notably azcentral.com) block automated access; their stories appear when they surface through search or syndication.

How the numbers were built

Statewide anchors. First full year: the Urban Institute's March 2026 projection that Arizona's expansion enrollment falls 123,000–246,000 in 2028 (central 197,000) under the January 1, 2027 work requirement and six-month renewals, plus AHCCCS's 22,263 advance notices for the October 1, 2026 immigrant-eligibility change (low 11,100 / high 34,300 including SSI cases under review). Full phase-in: RAND's June 2026 projection of 327,400 fewer AHCCCS enrollees in 2034 (work requirements 198,800; provider-tax retrenchment 94,200; renewals 65,700; immigrant and other 10,500), with KFF/CBO's ±24% Arizona uncertainty band as the low/high envelope. JLBC's own estimate — 171,000 fewer members by FY 2030 from the two eligibility provisions — falls inside the range.

District allocation. AHCCCS does not publish enrollment by legislative district or ZIP code, so statewide figures were allocated with Census ACS 2020–2024 tract data on adults 19–64 with Medicaid (excluding Medicare dual-eligibles and adults reporting a disability), mapped block-by-block to the 2022 IRC districts. American Indians — exempt from the work requirement under Sec. 71119 — were removed from that component using 2020 Census adult counts. The allocation was calibrated to AHCCCS's actual expansion enrollment by congressional district (calibration factors 0.90–1.12) and to county enrollment.

Deaths. 0.76 uninsured per enrollee lost (CBO). Then 1.2 / 2.2 / 3.6 additional deaths per year per 1,000 uninsured — Sommers, Long & Baicker (Annals of Internal Medicine 2014), Gaffney, Himmelstein & Woolhandler (Annals 2025) and Sommers (American Journal of Health Economics 2017) — spread across ages 19–64 by each district's age profile and Arizona's age-specific death rates. Steady-state annual figures; effects build over three to four years.

Life expectancy. CDC census-tract life expectancy (USALEEP, 2010–2015) aggregated to districts, shifted to the latest county figures (NCHS 2020–2022; statewide 76.7). The district's additional deaths are added to the Arizona 2022 life table at ages 19–64 and life expectancy at birth is recomputed. The change is a period life-table effect — what life expectancy would be if the excess mortality persisted — not years lost by each resident.

Hospital assessment step-down. Each facility's SFY 2025 assessment paid and assessment-funded payments received (coverage payments plus HEALTHII directed payments, from AHCCCS's June 2026 report) are scaled by the share of the 6.0% rate that H.R. 1 removes (41.7% at 3.5%), with federal matching held constant in constant dollars — the same method as the CSPA one-pager; statewide totals tie to it ($1.80B payments lost, $565M assessment saved, $1.24B net per year; $9.90B net over FFY 2028–2037). JLBC's alternative assumption (AHCCCS protects the coverage match and concentrates the cut in HEALTHII) gives $1.1B–$2.1B a year by FY 2032 and shifts losses toward hospitals with large HEALTHII payments. Facilities were assigned to districts by geocoding street addresses to 2020 census blocks; a hospital's patients and staff come from many districts. IHS and tribal (638) facilities are matched at 100% federal funds and are not assessment-funded; they are marked † and their $83M is included only to tie to the one-pager.

Verification. An independent audit re-derived every district figure from the raw data with separate code, confirmed all 13 spot-checked source claims against the live sources, and flagged two methodological problems (the American Indian exemption; district-scaled death rates) that were corrected in this version.

Caveats — read before quoting
  • District figures are allocations of statewide projections, not observed counts. Treat them as best estimates with ranges.
  • Low / Central / High combine the low and high ends of both the coverage-loss and mortality-effect evidence: a bounding envelope, not a confidence interval.
  • Exemptions for parents of children under 14 and the medically frail cannot be located geographically; family-heavy districts may be modestly overstated.
  • About 26% of the 2034 loss is RAND's assumption that Arizona trims optional eligibility as provider-tax revenue falls; JLBC does not project enrollment loss from that provision. The district panel shows the figure without it.
  • The uninsured conversion (0.76) is conservative relative to KFF's 290,000 Arizona estimate for 2034; deaths and life-expectancy effects would be about 17% larger using KFF.
  • Mortality evidence comes from studies of people gaining coverage; symmetry for losses is assumed, as in every published H.R. 1 projection.
  • Baseline life expectancy reflects 2020–2022 mortality, which is pandemic-depressed, especially in Apache, Navajo and Gila counties (LDs 6 and 7). The change barely depends on the baseline level.
  • Not included: ACA marketplace losses, hospital and clinic closures, provider payment cuts, SNAP-related effects. Enrollment already fell 9% (Aug 2025–Aug 2026) for reasons unrelated to H.R. 1 eligibility rules.
